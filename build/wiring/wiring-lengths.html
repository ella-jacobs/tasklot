How long does every wire need to be, and what goes where

Six RC522 readers hang under a 330 × 270 mm deck. Six of their seven wires are shared — that is the “parallel” part — and one, the chip select, is not. Below: the topology, a to-scale plan you can print at 100%, and a cut list in millimetres.

deck 330 × 270 mm 6 readers ~10.9 m of wire Elegoo Mega 2560

01 Read this before you cut anything

Three facts that change the numbers.

1. The reader slot is 4.90 mm total, and the board is 1.6 mm of that. That leaves 3.30 mm of clear space between the top of the PCB and the underside of the deck. A normal 2.54 mm pin header sticks up about 8.5 mm, and a female Dupont plug on top of it more. A vertical header facing the deck will not fit. Your two options are to solder the wires flat onto the pads (lowest, and it also gets the antenna closest to the car, which is where the read range comes from), or to fit a right-angle header pointing out the open end of the channel.

2. Print the fit-test holder first. It exists exactly for this — build/cad/exports/tasklot-reader-holder-glue.stl, ~30 min. Slide a real, wired RC522 in and see it seat before six holders get glued to a deck. The number the CAD is least sure of is reader_header_underside = 2.5 mm, which is still a guess — caliper the real board's underside and update the CAD parameter.

3. The Mega has one 3V3 pin, and you need to feed six readers from it. You physically cannot plug two wires into one header pin, so a junction is not optional. That is what the bus rail in the diagram is. It is also why the wire plan below is the shape it is.

02 The topology

What “in parallel” actually means here: six wires everybody shares, one wire each that nobody shares.

Six shared conductors (3V3, GND, SCK 52, MOSI 51, MISO 50, RST A0) run from the Mega to a bus rail, then branch once to each reader. The chip-select line is the only thing that makes reader 3 different from reader 4, and it gets its own wire back to its own Mega pin. IRQ is not connected on any reader.

Shared — 6 conductors

  • 3V3 → Mega 3V3. Never 5 V. It kills the reader instantly.
  • GND → any Mega GND
  • SCK → pin 52
  • MOSI → pin 51
  • MISO → pin 50
  • RST → A0

Not shared — 1 each

  • R1 space 1 → SS 44
  • R2 space 2 → SS 45
  • R3 space 3 → SS 40
  • R4 space 4 → SS 41
  • R5 record → SS 36
  • R6 drive-through → SS 37

Pins 44/45/40/41 are what firmware/history/tasklot_full already declares. 36 and 37 come from firmware/history/six_reader_check. Don't renumber them.

03 The plan, to scale

Deck coordinates in millimetres, origin at the front-left corner. Print at 100% and it is a 1:1 template.

The red bar on each holder is the open end of the U-channel. The RC522 slides in there and its wires leave from there — so that is the point every length below is measured from. The four parking holders are fixed by the laser deck's engraved guides; the record and drive-through holders are shown rotated 180° so their open ends face the Mega, which saves about 130 mm on each of their runs. That is free, with one catch: slide those two reader boards in before the Mega is bolted down, because the Mega sits right where your hands need to be.

The LCD is the one cable that will not reach on a stock jumper. The screen sits rear-centre, its cable drops through the deck hole at (132, 240), and the Mega is near the front. Allowing 60 mm inside the frame itself, that run wants about 475 mm × 4 wires (5 V, GND, SDA 20, SCL 21). Stock backpack cables are 200 mm and a long Dupont jumper is 300 mm — neither makes it. Cut this one from spool, or plan on a joint. Mega I2C is pins 20/21, not A4/A5.

06 How much wire to buy

10.9 m

all six readers + LCD, bus-rail plan. Buy 15 m and you have margin for mistakes.

7.5 m

four parking readers + LCD only — which is what firmware/history/tasklot_full actually runs today. See the power note below.

26 AWG stranded silicone is the right wire. Six readers draw 78–156 mA total, so 26 AWG is nowhere near its limit — the reason to pick it is that it bends without work-hardening every time the deck comes off. Solid core will snap at the solder joint after a handful of open-and-shut cycles, and this thing is going to be opened a lot before Monday. Ribbon cable is the fast alternative: peel six conductors off a 40-way rainbow and the colour code is done for you.

That saves roughly 1.4 m of wire and costs you this: each middle reader now has two wires on every shared pad instead of one — 13 joints on a board the size of a credit card — and one cold joint takes out every reader downstream of it rather than just its own. One day before the review, take the rail. You can pull a single reader off a rail to test it; you cannot do that to a chain.

08 The power question, honestly

Each RC522 draws 13–26 mA. Six of them is 78–156 mA, and the Mega's 3.3 V rail comes off a small regulator that is not rated for that with confidence. Four readers (52–104 mA) is the configuration that has actually been bench-tested and read through plywood.

  • Wire the rail for six, populate four. Cut and place all six branches, leave the record and drive-through ends unterminated. Adding them later is then a two-minute job, not a rebuild.
  • If you want all six live: add an AMS1117-3.3 module fed from the Mega's 5 V rail, tie its ground to the Mega's ground, and take the bus 3V3 from the module instead of the Mega pin. Every other wire stays exactly as drawn.
  • Put a 10–47 µF capacitor across 3V3 and GND at the far end of the rail. A metre of thin wire feeding six RF front ends benefits from it, and it costs nothing.
  • The symptom to know: a reader that answers VersionReg correctly but never sees a tag is a starved 3.3 V rail, not a broken bus. firmware/history/six_reader_check is the sketch that tells you which one you have.

09 One thing to settle before the walls get cut

Two files in the repo disagree about where the Mega goes, and only one of them ends up in plywood. It does not hold up the deck.

The printed CAD puts the Mega at mega_keepout_origin = [120, 10], so it occupies X 120–232. The laser front wall — uls_walls_sheet(), which is the walls sheet — has its 62 × 24 mm “Mega USB/power service opening” at X 250–312. Those don't overlap — an 18 mm gap at best, and more like 130 mm if the USB faces sideways. The Mega as placed cannot reach the hole cut for it.

It blocks nothing on the deck. TASKLOT-deck-sheet.pdf is deck-only and never calls front_wall(), so the deck sheet can be cut without this being answered. It only has to be settled before the walls run.

The hole is the half that gets lasered, so it is the half that can't move afterwards. If the Mega is repositioned to actually use it — roughly X 254–307, long axis front-to-back — it tucks under R1's holder with about 2.6 mm to spare. That figure assumes nothing hangs below the holder, which is true only if you follow section 01 and solder the reader wires flat. Fit a downward pin header and a Dupont plug instead and the lowest point drops to about Z 24.6, inside the opening's own 5–29 mm band and in the way of anything reaching through it. The two decisions are the same decision.

10 Files

  • build/wiring/deck-wiring-plan-1to1.svg — the plan above as a standalone file. Print at 100%, no scaling. It tiles across four sheets of Letter, or prints on one 11 × 17. Mirror it in X if you're laying it against the flipped-over deck.
  • build/cad/exports/tasklot-reader-holder-glue.stl — print this before anything else and check a wired RC522 seats.
  • build/cad/exports/tasklot-reader-holders-6up.stl — all six holders, 213 × 108 mm plate, ~3 h, no supports. It is six separate parts on one plate on purpose; the slicer will warn about disconnected geometry, and it is wrong.
  • build/laser/exports/sheet_1_underside_score.svg — the deck's engraved holder guides, which is what fixes the reader positions the lengths are measured from.
  • firmware/history/six_reader_check — upload it once the bus is soldered. It reports every reader's VersionReg, which is how you find a bad joint without a multimeter.
